SwiftSekai

Learn Swift and SwiftUI with an engaging macOS app for new developers: practical lessons, interactive exercises and projects, plus Taylor, a built-in AI assistant that answers questions and helps you troubleshoot.

01Discover

The problem

Problem statement

Self-taught programmers struggle to learn Swift effectively due to fragmented resources and lack of structured guidance. Many find it hard to locate beginner-friendly materials that combine theory with hands-on practice.

The need: a dedicated macOS platform with structured learning paths, interactive exercises, and accessible support.

02Research

Market & people

Market & competitor analysis

I analysed Hacking with Swift, Swift Playgrounds, Codecademy and Udemy. Hacking with Swift offers excellent free content but lacks interactive practice. Swift Playgrounds is great for beginners but limited to iPad. Codecademy is structured but lacks Swift depth. Udemy varies in quality and lacks community support.

The gap: no platform combined structured learning, interactive practice and real-time AI assistance in a native macOS environment.

15 self-taught learnersUser interviews

I interviewed 15 self-taught Swift learners about their learning journey, challenges and needs. Key pain points emerged:

  • “I don't know where to start.”
  • “I get stuck and have no one to ask.”
  • “I can't tell if I'm making real progress.”
03Define

Insights & personas

Key insights

Synthesising research and interviews revealed three critical needs:

  • Clear learning progression: a structured path from beginner to advanced.
  • Immediate assistance: getting stuck without help leads to frustration and abandonment.
  • Hands-on practice: passive learning doesn't build real coding skills.

Make Swift learning feel like having a personal mentor guiding you every step of the way.

Alex & MariaUser personas

Alex, a 25-year-old career switcher, wants to build iOS apps but feels overwhelmed by scattered resources. Maria, a 19-year-old computer science student, needs supplementary materials for her coursework. Both need structured guidance, hands-on practice and instant help, which shaped progress tracking, achievement badges and Taylor.

How might we…Design challenge

“How might we create a learning experience that combines the structure of a classroom, the flexibility of self-paced learning, and the support of a personal mentor?”

Three objectives followed: clear learning paths, instant help through AI, and engaging hands-on exercises.

04Develop

Structure

Four sectionsInformation architecture

The app is structured into four main sections:

  • Learn: structured lessons from basics to advanced.
  • Practice: interactive coding challenges.
  • Projects: real-world app building.
  • Taylor AI: an instant help assistant.

Each lesson follows one pattern: concept, code examples, interactive exercises, mini-project.

Lo-fi wireframes

Low-fidelity wireframes tested different approaches to lesson structure, code editor placement and AI assistant integration, focusing on intuitive navigation, a prominent editor, and easily accessible help.

05Test

Test & iterate

8 participantsUsability testing

I tested clickable lo-fi prototypes with 8 self-taught programmers: start a lesson, write code, ask the AI for help, track progress. Users couldn't see what came next, the AI assistant was hard to find, and the code editor felt too small.

Design iterations

  • Expanded the code editor with collapsible sidebars.
  • Moved the AI assistant from a separate window to an always-visible side panel.
  • Added a clear progress sidebar showing completed, current and upcoming lessons.
  • Implemented achievement badges to celebrate milestones.
06Deliver

Design system & hi-fi

Visual design system

The visual design embraces macOS Big Sur's design language: rounded corners, vibrant colours, translucent materials. Swift orange (#F05138) is the primary accent, with cool greys and whites as a professional backdrop. Typography uses SF Pro for consistency with macOS.

Three-panel layoutHi-fi prototype

A three-panel layout: lesson navigation (left), content and code editor (centre), and Taylor (right). Real-time syntax highlighting, progress indicators, achievement badges and smooth transitions create an engaging, game-like feel.

Core features

  • Taylor AI Assistant: explanations, code suggestions and debugging, without leaving your workflow.
  • Interactive Code Editor: write and run Swift with instant feedback.
  • Progressive Learning Path: basics to advanced, with clear milestones.
  • Real-World Projects: build actual iOS apps, not toy examples.
  • Achievement System: badges and progress tracking.
  • Offline Support: download lessons to learn anywhere.
07Impact

Results

12 participantsUser testing results

Final testing with 12 participants: 100% completed core tasks, and average task time dropped by 40% versus lo-fi testing. No critical usability issues were found.

It's like having a patient teacher who never gets frustrated with my questions.

87%completed the first three modules (vs. 15% industry average)
92%found Taylor AI helpful for getting unstuck
75%felt significantly more confident after two weeks
45minaverage daily engagement

50 learners · 4 weeksBeta

Beta testing with 50 self-taught programmers over 4 weeks. The combination of structured learning, hands-on practice and AI assistance reduced dropout and improved learning outcomes.

(08) Reflect / Key learnings

Design is problem-solving, not decoration.

Design is problem-solving, not decoration. SwiftSekai's success came from deeply understanding the problem (self-taught learners feeling overwhelmed and isolated) before jumping to solutions.

Iterate ruthlessly based on user feedback. The initial wireframes were quite different from the final design. Each validated iteration made the experience dramatically better.

Technology should enhance, not replace, human connection. Taylor doesn't try to replace a teacher; it provides instant help when you're stuck.

What I'd do differently: involve developers earlier to understand the technical constraints of the AI integration, and run longer-term studies on retention beyond the first weeks.
